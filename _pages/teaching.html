I am passionate about helping students learn to interpret and act on financial information — a skill that shapes decisions in nearly every leadership role. My approach emphasizes making dense financial material accessible without stripping away the nuance that gives it meaning. I draw on a variety of sources of information, such current earnings announcements, conference calls, and disclosure controversies to ground abstract accounting concepts in the choices companies actually face.

Corporate Financial Reporting

UC Berkeley, Haas School of Business

Rating: 6.4/7

This class empowers you not to just read, but to interpret and act on financial information, setting the stage for innovative strategies, stronger partnerships, and more effective leadership. No matter your career trajectory, the ability to decipher complex financial reports will set you apart as a leader in your future career. We will analyze the quantitative and qualitative information found in financial statements, ESG reports, conference calls, earnings announcements, and so much more. Throughout the semester, we’ll discuss the key disclosure rules in the United States, the communication methods available to managers, managers’ incentives and ability to exert discretion over reported earnings and the interplay between a company’s corporate strategy and its financial reporting policies and practices.

Introduction to Financial Accounting

University of Pennsylvania, The Wharton School

Rating: 3.44/4

This course provides an introduction to financial accounting and the analysis of financial statements. Topics include the accounting equation, accrual accounting, revenue recognition, and the mechanics of the balance sheet, income statement, and statement of cash flows. Building on these foundations, the course explores how firms communicate their financial position and performance to external users, and how differences in accounting choices and disclosures affect the information available to investors, creditors, and other stakeholders.
